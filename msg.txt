refactor: rename the layout convention to tin

The word "layout" named two different things and had worn out both. The
design idiom — the visual and spatial arrangement — already retired it in
favour of `surface`, `stage`, `flank`, `zone`, `air`. What survived was the
other sense: the chrome with a hole that `page` infers, a file that declared
its assets, its nav, a `contents` slot and a footer, and nothing else.

That sense now has its own word, and it pairs with `.design` the way the
files pair on disk: `[app].tin` is the modest metal over the building,
`[app].design` is how it looks, and the app's `[view].sp` files are what is
inside. One concern per file, each named for its concern.

Renamed: nine files to `[app].tin`; `Library#layout` to `Library#tin`;
`Library.from` resolves `[app].tin` beside the app's views, accepting either
the app directory or its `views/`; `merge_libraries(tin:)`;
`Builder#wrapped_in_tin`; the `:layout` convention to `:tin` with its marker
`wrapped_in_tin`; and the checkers, boot, and docs corpora, which globbed
`.sp` and would otherwise have silently stopped reading the nine frames.

A tin that does not exist is left a real answer rather than an error: roth's
views have none, and its page carries its own chrome, as `Library.tin_path`
returning nil records. That is also why the specs' in-memory tins report
themselves as `tin` in an error rather than a path — there is no file to
name, and inventing one would send a reader looking for it.

Verified: all nine gates green, suite 462 runs / 5,679 assertions with no
failures, every tin resolving, and both studio UIs rendering live.
